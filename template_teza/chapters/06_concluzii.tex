\section{Summary of Findings}
\label{sec:summary}

\textbf{Cross-dataset generalisation failure is systematic for single-source models.}
All six single-source models in Stage~1 achieve near-perfect in-distribution macro F1
(above 0.99 for Datasets~A and~C, 0.65 for Dataset~B) but collapse when tested on images
from a different dataset. The minimum off-diagonal macro F1 in the cross-dataset matrix is
0.1436; several cells reach exactly 0.3333, the value produced by a classifier that assigns
every input to a single class. The failure is consistent across both architectures
(ResNet-50 and ResNet-18) and both generation paradigms (diffusion and GAN). Because
near-identical failure occurs in a model with 2.2 times more parameters, architecture
capacity is not the primary cause. The evidence points instead to dataset-level shortcut
learning: models learn statistical properties specific to their training dataset rather
than generator artefacts that transfer across datasets.

\textbf{Data diversity is the primary driver of out-of-distribution robustness.}
The best single-source model achieves F1 of 0.6738 on the completely unseen Dataset~A
(trained on Dataset~B). Adding Dataset~C to the training corpus raises this to 0.7361 in
Stage~3. Including Dataset~A in the Stage~4 corpus brings it to 0.9936. Each improvement
correlates with the addition of a new real-image source, not with total data volume.
The Stage~4 combined corpus, 80,000 images from three real-image sources and six generator
families, produces near-perfect detection on Datasets~A and~C (F1 of 0.9936 and 0.9987
for ResNet-50). At this data scale, the ResNet-50 advantage over ResNet-18 grows to
0.016--0.028~F1 across all three test sets, while at small data scale the two architectures
are near-parity or even inverted. Architecture choice and training data composition are
therefore not independent: larger models benefit most when the training corpus is
sufficiently large and diverse.

\textbf{Difference of Gaussians preprocessing does not improve detection.}
Experiments~11 and~12 tested DoG preprocessing as a means to expose generative upsampling
artefacts by suppressing low-frequency content and colour. The results show consistent
degradation relative to the RGB baseline: ResNet-50 loses 0.005~F1 on Dataset~A, 0.011
on Dataset~B, and 0.013 on Dataset~C. Two mechanisms explain this. First, ResNet layers
initialised from ImageNet already function as frequency-selective filters that capture the
high-frequency information DoG is intended to expose. Second, the DoG transform discards
colour and low-frequency content that models rely on for in-distribution detection. The
net effect is negative.

\textbf{Dataset~B performance is limited by three compounding factors, not one.}
The best macro F1 on the Dataset~B test set is 0.5798 (ResNet-50, Experiment~13). Three
factors compound: five diffusion generators with distinct visual characteristics; MS-COCO
real images contributing only 17.5\% of Stage~4 training real images, versus 57.5\%
Shutterstock and 25\% FFHQ; and a 1:5 real-to-AI test set imbalance that amplifies real
class errors in the macro metric. The per-generator breakdown isolates the second factor
as the dominant cause: real recall is 0.61 across all five generator subsets, including
MidJourney~v6, which was excluded from training. If the missing generator were the primary
cause, only the MidJourney~v6 subset would show degraded real recall. The uniformly flat
0.61 across all five subsets points to a systematic misclassification of MS-COCO real
images, independent of which AI generator they are paired with.

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

\textbf{Quantitative 3$\times$3 cross-dataset generalisation matrix across two generation
paradigms.} The matrix covers all nine combinations of training and test dataset for both
ResNet-50 and ResNet-18, spanning diffusion-model and GAN generation paradigms. It
establishes that off-diagonal failure is not architecture-specific or paradigm-specific:
every off-diagonal cell falls substantially below the corresponding in-distribution value,
with the worst reaching 0.1436. This structure of the matrix constitutes direct
quantitative evidence for the dataset-specific shortcut hypothesis.

\textbf{Multi-stage training progression from failure to robust detection.} The
four-stage experimental design isolates the effect of each modification to the training
corpus and configuration. The progression from single-source training in Stage~1 through
balanced Dataset~B in Stage~2, combined B+C with DoG ablation in Stage~3, and the full
A+B+C corpus with advanced regularisation in Stage~4 traces a causal path from near-total
OOD failure to near-perfect in-distribution detection. The dominant factor in each
stage transition is the introduction of a new real-image source, rather than additional
AI-image volume or architecture change.

\textbf{Per-generator breakdown of Dataset~B failure.} The per-generator evaluation
uses the metadata CSV that accompanies Dataset~B to partition the test split by generator
label, computing F1 and per-class recall for each of the five generator subsets
independently. This methodology makes the uniform real recall pattern visible: aggregate
Dataset~B F1 of 0.5798 does not reveal that real recall is identically 0.61 across all
five subsets. The approach is directly applicable to any multi-generator benchmark with
per-sample provenance metadata and provides a cleaner diagnostic than aggregate metrics
alone.

\textbf{\texttt{dataset\_combined\_v2} training corpus.} The 80,000-image corpus
constructed for Experiments~13 and~14 draws from three real-image sources (Shutterstock,
FFHQ, MS-COCO) and six AI generator families across two paradigms. The real and AI
subsets are balanced at 40,000 each. MidJourney~v6 is withheld from training and
evaluated as an unseen-generator probe. The construction, including the semantic pairing
of Dataset~B real and AI images by MS-COCO caption and the proportional sampling from
each source, is described in Chapter~3 and in the code listed in the appendix.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future_work}

\textbf{Rebalancing the MS-COCO real-image proportion.} The most direct improvement
available for Dataset~B performance is to increase the MS-COCO contribution to the
combined training corpus. At 17.5\% of training real images, MS-COCO is substantially
underrepresented relative to its weight in the Dataset~B test set, where all 7,500 real
images are MS-COCO. Raising the MS-COCO proportion to 40\% (from 7,000 to approximately
16,000 real images), matched by a corresponding increase in Dataset~B AI images to
maintain class balance, would address the real-image distribution mismatch directly.
This rebalancing is expected to raise real recall from the current 0.61 ceiling; the
per-generator analysis shows no generator-specific floor on real recall, so the uniform
0.61 is a training-corpus artefact rather than an intrinsic difficulty. Including
MidJourney~v6 in training is a secondary improvement: AI recall on the MidJourney~v6
subset (0.43) is the only generator-specific metric substantially below the
trained-generator range (0.69--0.78). Adding approximately 1,750 MidJourney~v6 training
images (matching the per-generator count of the four included generators) would likely
close this gap without requiring a full corpus reconstruction.

\textbf{Scaling to larger architectures.} ResNet-50 outperforms ResNet-18 on the
combined training corpus by a consistent margin: 0.0162 on Dataset~A, 0.0284 on
Dataset~B, and 0.0124 on Dataset~C. At small data scale (Stage~1), this margin was absent
or inverted, with ResNet-18 achieving F1 of 0.5019 on Dataset~C OOD versus 0.3997 for
ResNet-50. The reversal at large scale suggests that the capacity advantage follows a
data-scale interaction rather than a simple monotonic relationship. Training ResNet-101
(${\approx}45$M parameters) on \texttt{dataset\_combined\_v2} under identical
hyperparameters would test whether the Dataset~B F1 continues to rise above 0.5798. If
it does, architecture scaling is a viable path alongside corpus rebalancing. If
ResNet-101 plateaus near the ResNet-50 result, the implication is that model capacity is
not the binding constraint at this data scale and that training corpus improvements would
have greater impact than additional parameters.

\textbf{Frequency-domain input.} The DoG experiments established that replacing RGB with
a band-pass representation is harmful: F1 dropped by 0.005 on Dataset~A, 0.011 on
Dataset~B, and 0.013 on Dataset~C for ResNet-50. A less destructive alternative is to
append the FFT magnitude spectrum as an additional fourth input channel alongside the
three RGB channels, rather than replacing them. This preserves all colour and
low-frequency information while making spectral structure explicitly available. Generative
models that use latent-space upsampling grids produce periodic spectral peaks at
frequencies determined by the upsampling stride; a fourth FFT channel exposes these peaks
directly without requiring the first convolutional layer to infer them from RGB. The key
distinction from DoG is that the classifier can learn to ignore the FFT channel if RGB
features are more discriminative, but cannot recover information that DoG has already
discarded. An ablation comparing RGB~(3~ch), RGB+FFT~(4~ch), and DoG~(3~ch) would
quantify the benefit precisely.

\textbf{Additional generation paradigms.} The current corpus covers the diffusion and GAN
paradigms across six generator families. Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architectures,
including the denoising transformer backbone used in Stable Diffusion~3 and several
recent open-source models, represent a third architectural family whose low-level artefact
profile differs from both U-Net-based diffusion and GAN upsampling. Adding a DiT-only
dataset, pairing its outputs with a held-out real-image source not already in
\texttt{dataset\_combined\_v2}, would test whether the multi-source approach extends to
a third paradigm without degrading performance on the existing two. Beyond paradigm
coverage, any fixed training corpus becomes stale as new generators are released. A
practical deployment pipeline would require monitoring per-generator F1 metrics on
incoming data, triggering retraining when any generator's performance drops below a
threshold, and maintaining a rolling corpus that incorporates recent generator outputs
alongside the historical training data.
