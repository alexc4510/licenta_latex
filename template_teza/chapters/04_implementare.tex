\chapter{Experiments and Results}
\label{ch:experiments}

The experimental investigation is organised into four stages of increasing data diversity
and training complexity. Each stage builds on the findings of the previous one, progressing
from single-source baselines through multi-source combined training with advanced
regularisation. All experiments use the datasets, architectures, and evaluation protocol
described in Chapter~3. Results are reported as macro-averaged F1-score on the held-out
test splits of Datasets~A, B, and~C.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\section{Stage 1: Single-Source Training (Experiments 1--6)}
\label{sec:stage1}

The first stage establishes baseline performance by training each architecture on a single
dataset in isolation. Six experiments cover all combinations of two models (ResNet-50 and
ResNet-18) and three datasets (A, B, C). All experiments use the standard training
configuration described in Section~\ref{sec:standard_config}: Adam optimiser
($\eta = 10^{-4}$), batch size~128, cross-entropy loss with inverse-frequency class
weights, and random horizontal flipping as the only data augmentation. Best checkpoints
are selected by highest validation accuracy.

Table~\ref{tab:stage1_results} summarises the in-distribution results. All six models
converge within four epochs, consistent with the use of ImageNet-pretrained weights.

The off-diagonal entries reveal near-complete generalisation failure. The minimum observed
value is 0.1436 (ResNet-18, trained on C, tested on B), well below the 0.33 baseline that
corresponds to predicting one class for the entire test set. Several cells reach exactly
0.3333, confirming that the model collapses to a single-class prediction on those
pairings. Table~\ref{tab:cross_dataset_matrix} presents all nine values for both
architectures.

The best off-diagonal transfer is 0.6738 (ResNet-50, trained on B, tested on A), reflecting
partial feature overlap between the diffusion-model outputs in Datasets~A and~B. The
failure pattern is consistent across architectures: ResNet-50 and ResNet-18 produce
qualitatively identical off-diagonal results, ruling out model capacity as the primary
explanation. The implications of this pattern are discussed in Chapter~5.

Two conclusions follow from Stage~1. First, in-distribution F1 is not a reliable proxy
for OOD performance: a model scoring 0.9961 on its own test set scores 0.1553 and 0.3333
on the other two. Second, the failure is not random but structured: the best OOD transfer
always occurs between the two diffusion-model datasets (A and B), while the GAN dataset
(C) produces the hardest OOD targets for models trained on either diffusion dataset.
Stage~2 investigates whether the Dataset~B difficulty stems from class imbalance.

\section{Stage 2: Balanced Dataset B (Experiments 7--8)}
\label{sec:stage2}

The 1:5 real-to-AI imbalance in Dataset~B (Section~\ref{sec:dataset_b}) is a candidate
cause of its relatively low Stage~1 performance. Experiments~7 and~8 test this by training
on a balanced version of Dataset~B: 7,000 real images paired with 7,000~AI images (1,400
per generator across five generators). Training configuration is otherwise identical to
Stage~1.

Each model is evaluated on both the original imbalanced test set and a balanced test split,
enabling direct comparison with Stage~1 baselines. Table~\ref{tab:stage2_results} presents
the results.

Balancing yields modest gains: $+0.031$ F1 for ResNet-50 and $+0.040$ for ResNet-18 on
the balanced test set. On the original imbalanced test, both models perform slightly worse
than their Stage~1 counterparts trained on the imbalanced distribution (0.6401
vs.\ 0.6512 for ResNet-50). Class imbalance thus accounts for a small part of Dataset~B
difficulty, but the fundamental challenge remains: five generators with distinct visual
characteristics are difficult to capture simultaneously with a single model.

These results show that the 1:5 imbalance in Dataset~B depresses the macro F1 metric but
is not the primary explanation for low performance. The F1 ceiling for a model trained
only on Dataset~B is approximately 0.67, regardless of how the imbalance is handled.
Stage~3 tests a different hypothesis: that combining training data from two datasets with
different real-image sources improves OOD robustness.

\section{Stage 3: Combined Datasets B+C (Experiments 9--12)}
\label{sec:stage3}

Stage~3 investigates whether combining training data from two datasets improves
out-of-distribution (OOD) generalisation. The training corpus is \texttt{dataset\_combined},
a 28,000-image balanced mixture: 14,000 real images (7,000 from Dataset~B's MS-COCO
subset and 7,000 from Dataset~C's FFHQ subset) paired with 14,000~AI images (7,000
from Dataset~B's five generators at 1,400 each, and 7,000 from Dataset~C's StyleGAN).
This corpus spans both diffusion-model and GAN generation paradigms.

There is no dedicated test split for \texttt{dataset\_combined}. All four experiments in
this stage are evaluated directly on the original test sets of Datasets~A, B, and~C.
Dataset~A is entirely unseen during training and is the primary OOD probe.

\subsection{Raw RGB (Experiments 9--10)}
\label{sec:stage3_rgb}

Experiment~9 (ResNet-50, RGB) achieves F1$\,{=}\,0.7361$ on the entirely unseen
Dataset~A, an improvement of $+0.062$ over the best Stage~1 cross-dataset transfer
(0.6738, ResNet-50 trained on B, tested on A). Combining datasets with different real
image sources (MS-COCO and FFHQ) forces the model to learn a broader representation
of real-image appearance. Dataset~C performance is maintained at 0.9904, as the C test
distribution is well represented in training. Dataset~B performance (0.6312) is slightly
lower than the Stage~1 baseline trained directly on B (0.6512), reflecting the reduced
proportion of Dataset~B images in the combined 28,000-image corpus.

Figure~\ref{fig:exp09_confusion_a} shows the confusion matrix for Experiment~9 on the
Dataset~A test set. Both classes are partially recovered: the model correctly classifies
the majority of real and AI images, but with non-trivial error rates in both directions.
This partial OOD recovery, achieved without any Dataset~A training images, demonstrates
that combining two real-image sources (MS-COCO and FFHQ) already broadens the model's
representation of real-image appearance towards the Shutterstock distribution.

across two real-image distributions simultaneously.

\subsection{Difference of Gaussians Preprocessing (Experiments 11--12)}
\label{sec:stage3_dog}

Experiments~11 and~12 repeat Stage~3 training using DoG-preprocessed inputs
(Section~\ref{sec:dog_preprocessing}). DoG results are consistently lower than the
corresponding RGB results across all three test sets and both architectures. The largest
drop appears on Dataset~C: 0.9776 (ResNet-50, DoG) vs.\ 0.9904 (ResNet-50, RGB),
a decrease of 0.013. On Dataset~A: 0.7316 (DoG) vs.\ 0.7361 (RGB), a decrease of 0.005.

The consistent performance drop indicates that band-pass filtering to expose upsampling
artefacts does not improve detection. The network already extracts relevant
high-frequency information from standard RGB inputs. Removing colour and low-frequency
content eliminates information that is diagnostic for distinguishing real-image
distributions, which outweighs any artefact-emphasis benefit. DoG preprocessing is
dropped for all subsequent experiments.

The degradation is largest on Dataset~C ($-0.013$ for ResNet-50, $-0.017$ for ResNet-18),
which contains StyleGAN faces. GAN artefacts are the intended target of the DoG
band-pass filter, so one might expect DoG to help most on Dataset~C. The opposite result
suggests that StyleGAN faces do not produce the strong periodic artefacts characteristic
of earlier transposed-convolution GANs. StyleGAN's style-based architecture and
progressive growing produce smoother spatial statistics; the artefacts that DoG is tuned
to expose are weaker or absent. The model therefore loses useful RGB signal (skin tone,
lighting, hair texture) without gaining corresponding artefact-signal, producing a net
loss.

Stage~4 takes a different approach: instead of preprocessing the input signal, the
training corpus is extended to cover all three datasets simultaneously, using advanced
regularisation to prevent overfitting on the larger 80,000-image training set.

\section{Stage 4: Combined A+B+C with Advanced Regularisation (Experiments 13--14)}
\label{sec:stage4}

Stage~4 trains on \texttt{dataset\_combined\_v2}, an 80,000-image corpus drawn from
all three datasets (40,000 real + 40,000 AI). Dataset construction is described in
Section~\ref{sec:combined_datasets}; MidJourney~v6 images are withheld from training and
serve as an unseen-generator probe at evaluation time. The advanced training configuration
(Section~\ref{sec:advanced_config}) introduces Focal Loss ($\gamma=2.0$, label smoothing
$\varepsilon=0.1$), extended data augmentation, linear LR warmup over the first two
epochs, adaptive LR scheduling via ReduceLROnPlateau (factor $0.5$, patience $3$),
gradient clipping (max\_norm $= 1.0$), and early stopping (patience $5$ on val~F1).

\subsection{Experiment 13: ResNet-50}
\label{sec:exp13}

Figure~\ref{fig:exp13_f1_curve} shows the validation F1-score per epoch. Training runs
for 19~epochs before early stopping terminates it at epoch~19 (five consecutive epochs
without improvement over the epoch-14 best).

\begin{figure}[h!]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.85\columnwidth]{figuri/exp13_resnet50_combined_v2/resnet50_f1_curve.png}
  \caption{Experiment~13 (ResNet-50): validation macro F1-score per epoch. The
           ReduceLROnPlateau scheduler halves the learning rate from $10^{-4}$ to
           $5\times10^{-5}$ at epoch~12, triggering the best checkpoint at epoch~14
           (val F1$\,{=}\,0.9933$).}
  \label{fig:exp13_f1_curve}

The LR warmup is visible in epochs~1--2, where the learning rate rises linearly from~0
to~$10^{-4}$. Validation F1 improves steadily to 0.9910 at epoch~8. Three consecutive
non-improving epochs (9, 10, 11) trigger ReduceLROnPlateau, which halves the learning
rate to $5\times10^{-5}$ at epoch~12. The finer gradient updates allow the model to
break out of the plateau: F1 rises to 0.9928 at epoch~13 and 0.9933 at epoch~14,
the best checkpoint. Epochs~15--19 show further oscillation with no improvement;
a second LR reduction to $2.5\times10^{-5}$ at epoch~18 produces no further gains.

Figure~\ref{fig:exp13_loss_curve} shows training and validation loss. Training loss
decreases monotonically throughout; validation loss mirrors the F1 oscillation pattern,
confirming that the post-epoch-8 plateau is a generalisation issue rather than
insufficient training.

Figure~\ref{fig:per_generator_resnet18} shows the same per-generator breakdown for
Experiment~14 (ResNet-18). The relative ordering of generators is preserved: DALL-E~3
remains the easiest target and MidJourney~v6 the hardest. ResNet-18 achieves slightly
lower per-generator F1 than ResNet-50 for the four trained generators (differences of
0.0027--0.0266), but a marginally higher F1 on MidJourney~v6 (0.5335 vs.\ 0.5192). The
cross-architecture consistency confirms that the per-generator hierarchy reflects
properties of the generators themselves rather than architecture-specific features.

Among trained generators, DALL-E~3 is the easiest for both models (ResNet-50
F1$\,{=}\,0.6974$, ResNet-18 F1$\,{=}\,0.6776$). SD~2.1 is the hardest trained generator
(ResNet-50 F1$\,{=}\,0.6515$), likely due to its stylistic proximity to the Stable
Diffusion~v1.5 images in Dataset~A, which the model encounters as real-class training data.

MidJourney~v6, excluded from training, produces F1$\,{=}\,0.5192$ for ResNet-50 and
0.5335 for ResNet-18, near chance level on the balanced per-generator evaluation. The
model cannot reliably detect a generator it has never seen.

A key pattern in Table~\ref{tab:per_generator} is that real recall is approximately 0.61
across all five generators, including MidJourney~v6. If the primary cause of low
Dataset~B performance were the exclusion of MidJourney~v6 from training, real recall
would degrade specifically for the MidJourney~v6 subset and remain higher for the
four trained generators. The uniform ${\approx}\,0.61$ real recall across all generators
instead points to a systematic difficulty with the MS-COCO real image distribution,
which comprises only 17.5\% of the real images in the training corpus. This observation
is central to the analysis presented in Chapter~5.
